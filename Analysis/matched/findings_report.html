What drives the value of stochastic line planning?

A controlled comparison of two reference networks — SiouxFalls (sparse, hierarchical) and Mumford0 (dense, grid-like). All sweeps share identical operational parameters and the same load factor (ρ=0.10), so every comparison is confounder-free.

Metric: VSS (value of the stochastic solution) and EVPI (value of perfect information), each relative to the recourse cost RP. Aggregates are means over cases.

Topology does not set the level of value

The headline native gap (~40×) is a load and parameter artefact. Once both networks run at the same load factor and parameters, the value of stochastic planning is comparable — Mumford0 is even slightly higher at most k.

Replanning cost is the dominant lever

Of every parameter tested, the cost of replanning moves the value by orders of magnitude — in both networks. All other levers are negligible by comparison.

Disruption probability has a threshold

VSS and EVPI are flat over a wide range, then split sharply near certainty (p≥0.9): the nominal-day plan becomes wrong (VSS jumps) while perfect information loses value (EVPI collapses). Identical pattern in both networks.

Load factor governs the value

Stochastic planning pays off most in lightly-loaded networks; under congestion, deterministic bypass overflow dominates and plan choice stops mattering. The sparse network declines more steeply (it congests earlier).

Same mean, different risk profile

At costly replanning the two networks have a similar mean VSS but very different distributions: the dense network absorbs most disruptions (≈0) yet a minority are catastrophic (up to ~100% of RP); the sparse network is uniformly sensitive.

VSS and EVPI emphasise different regimes

The two measures are not interchangeable. The replanning-cost regime is VSS-dominated (a good first-stage plan matters more than perfect information); the congestion regime is EVPI-comparable.
